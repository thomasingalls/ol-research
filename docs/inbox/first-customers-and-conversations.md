## 2. Each lead

### 2.1 A relative's intellectual-property law firm

A relative of the user (a cousin, the user thinks) runs an intellectual
property law firm based in Paris, with an office in Singapore and
perhaps one more.

- **What it could give:** the user's point, a conversation about product
  shape with people whose work is searching. Prior-art search is
  professional set-searching of the kind these notes keep returning to:
  exhaustive, costly to get wrong, judged by what was missed
  ([the set builder](recall-accounted-set-builder.md)'s `missing()`).
- **Where it sits in the scope:** patents are in scope in principle,
  though not in the corpus yet; the patent test collections are kept, not
  dropped ([retrieval benchmarks](retrieval-benchmarks.md), P). A law
  firm is outside the wedge of academics and researchers, which makes it
  a conversation about shape more than a first customer, and a view of
  where the horizon leads. Its own documents are the "internal
  documents" the user counts in scope.
- **Europe and Asia:** offices in Paris and Singapore fit a Europe-first
  preference ([product shapes](product-shapes.md) §9, P) and look beyond
  it.
- **What to learn:** how they search now and with what; what a missed
  reference costs them; what "faithfully" would have to mean before they
  relied on a taught method ([teach it your method](teach-it-your-method.md)
  §6.2); what they pay for today.

### 2.2 DeepDyve

- **What the notes already hold:** a rental service with over 35 million
  full-text streaming papers as of 2026-01-01, publisher partners
  including Springer, Wiley and Oxford University Press, Elsevier not in
  its collection, and its own AI chat over the collection
  ([industry landscape](industry-landscape.md), S). The user's earlier
  assessment: it "has stayed afloat through cunning, cost-effective
  product choices but is slow, with some eighteen years of technical debt
  on a small team" (same note, P); and, on Clarivate's full-text reader,
  "they could have just bought DeepDyve"
  ([science-assistant landscape](science-assistant-landscape.md) §2, P).
- **What it could give, as the user sees it:** existing relationships
  with publishers; knowledgeable people; a location near UC Berkeley and
  Stanford, and a chief executive with a good network there. The user's
  impression is that it might be looking for an exit, and that a deal
  could help some friends; that is the user's reading, not something the
  notes can confirm.
- **Why publishers matter here:** full text is the gold standard, and a
  goal ([retrieval benchmarks](retrieval-benchmarks.md), P); methods-level
  criteria live in full text ([teach it your method](teach-it-your-method.md)
  §8). The industry note records that machine use is a separate right,
  priced by publishers, apart from an intermediary's margin on reading
  ([industry landscape](industry-landscape.md), the table of routes to
  text, P). What DeepDyve's agreements permit for machine use is the
  first question.
- **What to learn:** what its publisher agreements cover; which of its
  people know the publishers' side; what shape of arrangement, if any,
  makes sense. Any deal is the user's to weigh, and friends are involved,
  which calls for the usual care.

### 2.3 The neighbourhood around Université Paris-Saclay

The user lives beside Université Paris-Saclay, knows researchers among
the parents of their son's schoolmates, and could find student beta
testers nearby.

- **What it could give:** the wedge itself, academics and researchers,
  close enough for conversations in person and for watching someone
  search. Europe-first again ([product shapes](product-shapes.md) §9).
- **What to test with them:** the pitch ([teach it your method](teach-it-your-method.md)),
  on a question of their own. The 2025 joint position on AI in evidence
  synthesis asks reviewers to pilot a tool within their own review
  (that note §6.2); a researcher's own question is that pilot, and their
  marks are an answer key independent of the tool
  ([evaluation plan](../evaluation-plan.md) §3.4).
- **Breadth:** researchers here will not all be biomedical, and some will
  write in French; neither is a reason to leave them out.
- **Beta testers are users.** The serving plan notes that the query log
  holds what people typed, "fine while the only user is the user, and
  worth a second look before anyone else is one" ([serving plan](../serving-plan.md)
  §11, item 37, P). Students testing it are the first "anyone else":
  what is logged, what they are told, and what they agree to comes first.

## 3. What to take into the first conversations

What is there to show today: search over the corpus index on the box,
facet counts, the graph; the evaluation not yet run; none of the tools in
[the set builder](recall-accounted-set-builder.md),
[suggested cuts](suggested-cuts.md), [the shape](shape-of-a-result-set.md)
or [teach it your method](teach-it-your-method.md) built. So the first
conversations are for listening:

- how they search now, with what, and where it fails them;
- how they judge whether a search found enough;
- what they would need before relying on a tool: evidence it is no worse
  than what they do now, reported so it can be checked (O'Connor et al.,
  in [teach it your method](teach-it-your-method.md) §6.2);
- whether they would teach a tool their method, and publish it;
- what they pay for today, and who pays.
